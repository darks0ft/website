**My album [*Ultraview*](/discography/ultraview/) is about what happens when you take a step back and view life from afar. Your perspective shifts. With detachment comes revelation. And when you return, things don't look the same as before.**

I see *Ultraview* as a direct response to my previous album, [*Everydayness*](/discography/everydayness/). Whereas *Everydayness* explored the mundane everyday life on the ground, *Ultraview* takes a bird's-eye view.

Like ultraviolet light existing just beyond the visible spectrum, *Ultraview* attempts to transcend everyday life and reach some sort of ethereal summit. From there, I hope listeners can float, see things for what they are, make connections and find some resolve. It's probably my most intentionally uplifting album to date.

## The artwork: the Brocken spectre

The album artwork is inspired by a [Brocken spectre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocken_spectre), a natural optical phenomenon that creates multicolored halos around a person's shadow.

It often appears to hikers high in the mountains when the sun, clouds and atmospheric conditions align. The shadow seems suspended in mid-air, surrounded by glowing rings. I think it captures the album's recurring themes of distance, perspective and revelation.

I worked with the incredibly talented artist [Jameson Martin](https://www.jamesonmartinart.com) for the design. He did my last album cover as well.

## The concept: transcend and transform

Conceptually, the name and theme for *Ultraview* is inspired by the [overview effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overview_effect), a sort of out-of-body phenomenon astronauts say they experience when they leave Earth and can view the entire celestial body from space. Some report it's permanently perspective-shifting.

It reminds me of what [Carl Sagan said](https://www.planetary.org/worlds/pale-blue-dot) about Earth as a pale blue dot: "On it everyone you love, everyone you know, everyone you ever heard of... lived out their lives."

I went with the name *Ultraview* because it sounded more psychedelic and evocative than *Overview*. Interestingly, after settling on the title, I discovered [a paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/11/8/418) by anthropologist Deana Weibel, who also uses "ultraview" to describe the life-changing experience some astronauts report when gazing into an unobstructed field of stars. I loved that coincidence.

Of course, most of us aren't astronauts. We're stuck down here on the ground. But we can still find ways to gain distance: traveling, zoning out, looking down from an airplane window, taking mind-altering substances or simply taking a walk. Sometimes, the best revelations come when we wander away from whatever we're trying to figure out.

Some revelations arrive only after we've physically left. Why do we often find exactly what we wanted to say only after leaving a conversation? The French call this "[*l'esprit de l'escalier*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27esprit_de_l%27escalier)," or "the spirit of the staircase." I built "[Out Of Reach](/lyrics/ultraview/out_of_reach/)" around that idea.

Sometimes, taking a more cosmic perspective makes your individual problems seem insignificant in the grand scheme of things. Earth is about 4.5 billion years old. The universe is about 13.8 billion years old. We are ants. You get the picture.

But detachment goes both ways. Distance can also remind you of what's most important. After a long trip, I appreciate my home and family more than ever. There's a strange renewed appreciation, but also an uncanny feeling when returning home. It's funny how even the walls can look different.

## The listener's journey

Sonically and thematically, I tried to create a real arc for the listener: stepping away, gaining altitude, reaching a moment of clarity and eventually coming back down to earth.

The opening tracks, "[Stepped Away](/lyrics/ultraview/stepped_away/)," "[All Who Wander](/lyrics/ultraview/all_who_wander/)" and "[Out Of Reach](/lyrics/ultraview/out_of_reach/)," deal with detachment: taking that initial step back, beginning to wander and losing touch with wherever you came from.

Midway, the songs become more about processing the journey. "[Take It Or Leave It](/lyrics/ultraview/take_it_or_leave_it/)" is about seeing things for what they are. "[Bird's-Eye View](/lyrics/ultraview/birds_eye_view/)" is an instrumental, and I picture we're up in the clouds at this point, looking down at the small terrain below.

When you wander, unexpected things happen. You might even say they come "[Out Of The Blue](/lyrics/ultraview/out_of_the_blue/)."

"[What You Make Of It](/lyrics/ultraview/what_you_make_of_it/)" is the pinnacle of the journey, when you realize life is what you make of it. You make your own reality, the truth is within, there is no spoon, yadda yadda.

"[Outside Looking In](/lyrics/ultraview/outside_looking_in/)" is another instrumental, giving us space for processing and reflection. It has a medley-like quality, with guitar parts that invert some licks from my song "[Somewhere Nowhere](/lyrics/relativism/somewhere_nowhere/)" ([*Relativism*](/discography/relativism/), 2024).

From there, "[Whatever May Come](/lyrics/ultraview/whatever_may_come/)" is about accepting the unknown and whatever comes next. It has some pretty heavy cowbell.

Finally, all good things come to an end: walks, trips, shows, lives and even music albums. So "[All Good Things](/lyrics/ultraview/all_good_things/)" is the fitting closer.

## The sound and production

As on previous Darksoft albums, I recorded all parts using real instruments at home in my basement studio. You can read more about my process for [making a song at home](/blog/how-i-make-a-dream-pop-song-at-home/), [recording guitars](/blog/why-i-record-dream-pop-guitars-clean/) and [layering vocals](/blog/how-i-record-vocals-for-dream-pop-and-shoegaze/) on my blog.

I was really happy to feature Jacob Tessier on drums in "Bird's-Eye View" and shaker in "Whatever May Come." He's been a longtime drummer in the live band, has been on a number of tours, and is one of my favorite people.

For the mix, I worked with [Brian Fisher](https://briankfisher.com/work), the highly skilled engineer out of Boston. He mixed and mastered the album. As in previous releases, this involved re-amping guitar parts and adding some reinforcement samples and edits here and there. He's been a phenomenal collaborator.

Overall, I'm proud of this record. I intentionally tried to stay on the lighter side and make it more uplifting than some of my other records. It carries more of a major key tone throughout.

It's sweet yet bittersweet, chill but drivey, ordinary but dreamy, spiritual yet corporeal. Hopefully, it gives you a little distance from everything, like an out-of-body experience.

I created an [inspiration playlist for *Ultraview*](https://open.spotify.com/playlist/3PkxATbx5ezLnYxB3n058g). It has some of the tones, effects and styles I was thinking of when I wrote this and was preparing to have it mixed.

## The view from the clouds

I'll exit on one thought that only came to me after finishing this project.

When the world was less mapped and connected, there was an excitement in the great unknown. There was a kind of primordial serendipity that naturally threw people into distant lands or into one another like particles in the wind. Sometimes, getting lost was how you found something new.

Now, the world is less random. Earth has been charted. Technology tells us where to go, what to do and who to meet. Things are nicely cataloged, tagged, planned and packaged. Everyone is in a lane.

I hope one message from *Ultraview* is to remember to put it all down sometimes, step away and, if you have the means, make space to wander into the unknown. You might just reach a new high you never knew existed.
